# MAGIC: scale invariance, spectral evolution and generalization
Research notes — 2026-11-27. Consolidated post hoc analysis of the completed experiments, not a new training run.

## Executive summary
The MAGIC Gamma Telescope experiments separate three phenomena often conflated in the marginality hypothesis: radial parameter symmetry, optimizer-dependent spectral dynamics, and generalization/calibration. In ordinary SGDM, increasing the learning rate or extending training lowers FC2 alpha, but can worsen validation performance. Conventional coupled weight decay improves generalization at high learning rates while shifting alpha below 2. Enforcing exact radial symmetry in an MLP with learned scalar gains changes the SGDM dynamics markedly; the best validation-selected SGDM configuration has alpha 1.648, not 2, and lower test accuracy than the ordinary control. AdamW remains near 2 in both parameterizations. Frozen-checkpoint temperature calibration removes most of the enormous cross-entropy penalty from the normalized models, but cannot repair their classification errors or establish that gains alone cause the discrepancy.

The strong claim "radial scale invariance by itself implies alpha = 2 and optimal generalization" is not supported by these experiments. A narrower hypothesis linking marginality to a specific symmetry-respecting RG flow with appropriate regularization and time scale remains to be tested.

## 5. Explicit radial symmetry and calibration
Write an effective layer matrix as A_l = g_l sqrt(n_l) W_l / ||W_l||_F, with independent learnable scalar gain g_l, output dimension n_l, nonzero W_l and unchanged bias. Gains initialized to reproduce ordinary effective matrices; no spectral-shape penalty and WD=0. Positive rescaling W_l → b_l W_l leaves A_l, logits and loss unchanged at fixed gain and bias. This is parameter-space radial symmetry, not input-scale invariance and not a guarantee of regularization. Normalization multiplies every Gram eigenvalue by a common scalar (provided g_l≠0), so it does not directly impose an exponent. Fixed gains would constrain the effective Frobenius norm; learned gains do not.

## 6. Theoretical interpretation: symmetry is not the full RG flow
Let r = ||W||_F, U=W/r, and L(W,g)=ell(U,g). With Frobenius inner product, differentiating positive radial invariance gives <W,grad_W L>=0 and grad_W L(bW,g)=b^{-1}grad_W L(W,g). More explicitly, grad_W L = (I-UU^T) grad_U ell / r in vectorized coordinates. For plain SGD, small-step angular dynamics therefore have scale eta/r^2; finite updates give r_next^2 = r^2 + eta^2 ||grad_W L||^2 because of tangency. Thus even exact function symmetry allows raw norm drift. Momentum adds history, and trainable gains, biases and global gradient clipping introduce additional scales. The joint map W→bW, eta→b^2 eta is equivariant for an isolated unclipped SGD matrix with other state transformed appropriately, not automatically for a whole network using a shared learning rate. The nominal eta/r^2 column is not the actual SGDM or AdamW angular step.

For ordinary coupled L2 decay the objective L + lambda ||W||^2/2 is not radial invariant at fixed lambda. The regularizing gradient lambda W scales like b rather than b^{-1}; joint covariance would require lambda→b^{-2}lambda in the isolated SGD argument. This is a relevant symmetry-breaking perturbation in the dimensional sense, not a demonstrated RG eigenoperator. For the normalized architecture, a penalty on the raw radial norm is particularly different from a functional penalty on the learned gains: it changes the gauge radius and angular dynamics without directly limiting effective weight scale.

AdamW with zero decay remains adaptive Adam. Under a fixed raw matrix rescaling the first and second gradient moments scale as b^{-1} and b^{-2}, respectively, if their histories are correspondingly transformed. Their normalized ratio is approximately invariant when epsilon is negligible, unlike SGD's inverse scaling. Consequently the effective angular learning-rate scaling and joint covariance differ. Exact radial gradient orthogonality does not imply orthogonality of the preconditioned update, volume preservation, a zero trace-log correction, or an alpha=2 fixed point. A Jacobian/ERG derivation must explicitly include the update state, gain and bias degrees of freedom, clipping, finite-step effects and any decay. A density proportional to lambda^{-alpha} has a marginal first spectral moment at alpha=2 in the ideal unbounded-tail limit; finite matrices and finite cutoffs are not themselves a proof of that limit.

## 7. Conclusions and proposed next controlled experiment
Evidence supports learning-rate- and decay-dependent spectral evolution and a sizable calibration component of the normalized MLP's CE deficit. It does not establish that proximity to alpha=2 predicts the best model or that exact parameter symmetry is sufficient for good generalization. The normalized SGDM sweep also changes optimization geometry and learned gains, so it is not an isolated causal test of symmetry alone. Compare all configurations, including alpha>6 where short tails and cutoff choice merit sensitivity analysis; no new alternative spectral fits are asserted here.

Proposed, not executed: freeze each gain at its matched initialization value (possibly compare a second arm with gain-only regularization). Keep raw matrices normalized with identical data, seeds, width and update cap; use matched-LR comparisons alongside separately validation-tuned configurations. Confirm exact radial invariance, report per-layer raw/effective norms, gain and logit RMS, actual angular update size and radial update component, CE/accuracy/AUC and calibration, FC2 alpha/D/tail, seed uncertainty and failures. Fit temperatures only on held-out validation data and do not select hyperparameters by alpha. Predeclare an adequate tail-size sensitivity analysis. Use validation-only for iterative method development and obtain an independent test set or dataset before making a general performance claim. Fixed gains are a capacity/optimization intervention, not just a gauge choice: they could underfit, so retain learned-gain controls and treat their performance as an empirical question.

This report does not authorize additional training, merge experimental branches, overwrite checkpoints, or revise the theory paper. The archived experiments remain separately reproducible.
