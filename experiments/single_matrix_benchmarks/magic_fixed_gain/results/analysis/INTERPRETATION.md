## 3. Gain growth, calibration and generalization

The effective matrix is A=g sqrt(d_out) W/||W||_F. Hence ||A||_F=|g| sqrt(d_out), and its eigenvalues are raw W^T W eigenvalues times g² d_out/||W||_F². Rescaling W changes the raw spectrum's scale but not a scale-equivariant Pareto exponent at a given state. Freezing g is a functional restriction, not a pure gauge change: it also alters capacity, gradient magnitudes and the entire optimization path. Biases remain trainable.

At SGDM .03, the mean learned FC2 effective Frobenius norm is 146.67 versus 16.17 frozen; FC3 gain 5.249 versus .5722; validation logit RMS 77.62 versus 4.456; full fitted temperature 13.26 versus 1.237; raw ECE .1363 versus .02965. Train accuracy falls from .9966 to .9070 with freezing while validation accuracy rises from .8466 to .8687. The learned validation CE rises from .3569 at 2k to 1.9595 at 20k, whereas fixed CE stays .3222 to .3180. Temperature calibration reduces the learned 20k CE to .3739, but frozen raw CE .3180 and cross-fitted CE .3117 are still lower; AUC also improves. Thus gain growth and overconfidence account for a large portion of the learned-gain degradation, while the residual calibrated CE, ranking and accuracy differences indicate that the intervention is not merely a scalar temperature correction. The all-layer freeze does not identify which layer causes the effect, nor whether norm constraint versus altered optimization is the dominant cause. Validation estimates only.

For AdamW .0001 learned versus fixed, FC2 effective norm 53.79 versus 16.17, FC3 gain 1.9107 versus .5722, logit RMS 17.53 versus 4.257, fitted temperature 3.698 versus 1.199, validation AUC .9075 versus .9261. The same gain/overconfidence phenomenon occurs for AdamW, not just SGDM. Across the seven endpoint rows fixed raw CE lies .3139–.3220 and calibrated CE .3088–.3214. Learned-gain raw CE varies far more (.6409–2.2547). The fixed models' lower training accuracy is consistent with reduced memorization and capacity, not necessarily a superior unconstrained optimizer.

## 4. Spectral marginality and the original AdamW puzzle

Low-rate ordinary and learned-gain AdamW have near-2 alpha at 20k (2.027 and 2.051). Holding gains fixed moves the same AdamW .0001 endpoint to 1.776, with better validation metrics. At .0003, fixed alpha falls further to 1.465, with 87.00% accuracy. The best mean validation accuracy among the fully paired fixed-grid configurations is SGDM .1 (87.319%), which has alpha 1.645, not 2. SGDM .03 is near 2 (1.877) but has lower accuracy (86.873%). Thus neither architectural scale invariance nor good validation performance requires alpha=2 in this finite experiment. Do not select a model by alpha, and do not assert a universal correspondence.

SGDM .03 mean raw alpha at steps 0/2k/5k/10k/15k/20k: 10.748/3.930/3.237/2.016/1.892/1.877. AdamW .0001: 10.748/3.107/1.964/1.871/1.823/1.776. AdamW reaches the near-2 regime earlier in update count at these particular rates, but both drift below. For SGDM .03 the individual 20k fits are 1.8211 (D .04298, 24 tail values) and 1.9338 (D .05119, 47); the nominal alpha mean interval half-width is .7164. At 10k tails shrink to 21 and 19; the apparent transition coincides with a large change in fitted support. The corresponding 20k ordinary/learned means have 273/274.5 tail eigenvalues versus 35.5 fixed. Low D does not establish genuine heavy-tail behavior. Across all 83 fixed records D=.02526–.13363 and tail=15–270; at endpoints raw alpha=1.4612–4.0344, D=.02871–.10064 and tail=15–270.

Cross-optimizer comparisons within frozen architecture, paired on the two seeds (exploratory, not selection): SGDM .03 minus AdamW .0001 gives accuracy +.276 ± 2.171 pp, CE +.000887 ± .004579, calibrated CE -.000755 ± .003116, AUC +.000991 ± .004664, alpha +.10157 ± .92912. ## 5. Radial and angular updates, clipping and scale covariance

For L(bW)=L(W), g_W=∇_W L is orthogonal to W and g_{bW}=g_W/b for b>0. For an unclipped SGD momentum state transformed as v_b=v/b, LR must transform as eta_b=b² eta; for Adam with transformed moments m_b=m/b and v_b=v/b², epsilon_b=epsilon/b, LR must transform as eta_b=b eta. These are covariance laws of the idealized local update, not proofs of volume preservation or of an ERG trace-log identity. The Adam diagonal preconditioner is anisotropic and neither momentum nor preconditioning guarantees the realized step is tangent. The previous frozen-state intervention directly established unclipped covariance and clipping-induced failure under radial scaling; see [step audit](https://github.com/CalculatedContent/rg_optimizers/tree/ab1b7a686bb23e4edc0b0faaf4d5ee75064b6bb4/experiments/single_matrix_benchmarks/magic_scale_invariant/optimizer_step_audit) and [interpretation](https://github.com/CalculatedContent/rg_optimizers/blob/a510b1729d010120dba464c00d99170468015fa8/experiments/single_matrix_benchmarks/magic_scale_invariant/optimizer_step_audit/ANALYSIS.md).

Across all 83 fixed-gain probe records, the maximum across layer relative gradient radial cosines is 6.12e-7; endpoint sampled FC2 update radial cosines are positive .000478–.02941, angles 4.87e-5–.002366 rad. Mean sampled endpoint angle for SGDM .03 is .000552 rad; AdamW .0001 .000745 rad despite 300x smaller nominal LR; these are single sampled updates, not cumulative angular distance. FC2 raw norm at these settings is 17.294 and 17.836 despite almost identical constrained effective norm 16.172. Each complete fixed-gain run clipped only 1–3 of its 20,000 updates; average clipping factor exceeds .99998 at the displayed precision (actual means approximately .99998–1). The partial run clipped three times through 15k. This sharply contrasts with the prior learned-gain AdamW .0001 endpoint diagnostic where all five next-batch probes clipped (mean factor .25124), but those are separate saved-state probes, not comparable cumulative rates. The near absence of actual clipping in fixed-gain training means frequent clipping is not needed for the observed alpha differences here. It does not isolate clipping's causal contribution in the learned-gain runs. Causal identification would require a different authorized intervention, not a replay.

## 6. High alpha, short tails and negative sensitivity result

All 16 fixed-grid records with alpha>6 comprise the 14 duplicated initialization records (two unique initial states, alpha 14.122 and 7.375) and two SGDM .003 records at 2k (7.247 and 6.659). None at 20k. The initial raw tail spans are only 1.204 and 1.436 in eigenvalue ratio; largest/second-largest ratio about 1.004; not convincing outlier fingers. At 2k the same two SGDM spectra have top gaps 3.138 and 2.901. The learned-gain .003 endpoint alpha values 8.065 and 8.344 have 39 and 31 tail values, raw tail span 2.198 and 2.258.

The actual pinned WeightWatcher fix_fingers='clip_xmax' implementation was not executable in the diagnostic environment. Instead, a separate deterministic spectral-only sensitivity was carried out using all archived high-alpha spectra (26 representative records across architectures, 130 fits; repeated step-zero spectra collapsed within each architecture/seed). Remove exactly k=0,1,2,3,5 largest eigenvalues, refit a continuous Pareto with >=20 tail values and two-sided KS cutoff selection. No alpha-based selection. [Method and reproduction code](HIGH_ALPHA_SENSITIVITY.md) and [all results](high_alpha_trim_sensitivity.csv). For fixed spectra, trimmed alpha range 4.701–18.408; ordinary 4.720–18.408; learned 7.504–19.342. No fit is within [1.75,2.25]. Learned .003 endpoints remain steep under all trims (alpha 7.741–13.701 including k=0); many trims worsen KS. This negative result argues against a simple top-finger explanation for high alpha in these cases, but is not a replacement for the official WW flag. No corrected alpha substituted for any raw result.

## 7. Conclusion and limitations

This experiment supports a narrower mechanism: trainable scalar gains enable substantial effective norm/logit growth and validation overconfidence; constraining them produces more modest train accuracy, better held-out validation calibration/ranking and competitive accuracy for both optimizers. The puzzling AdamW alpha near 2 is not intrinsic to architectural scale invariance or AdamW alone: fixing gains at initialization shifts its spectral trajectory below 2 while validation performance improves. SGDM near-2 can coexist with competitive accuracy, but a below-2 SGDM rate yields higher observed accuracy. This is a useful counterexample to strong universal interpretations of spectral marginality. The observations do not establish an RG beta function, trace-log condition, causal effect of clipping or preconditioning, or a monotonic alpha-generalization law. Two seeds, one fixed validation partition, finite training, short and changing fitted tails, different functional constraints, missing endpoint, and no independent test access limit the inference. No new experiment is proposed as an authorized launch here.
